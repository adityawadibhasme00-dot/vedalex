WHO Traditional Medicine Strategy 2014-2023 - Global Framework

The WHO traditional medicine strategy 2014-2023 (ISBN 9789241506090, 76 pages, published 15 May 2013) was developed and launched in response to the World Health Assembly resolution WHA62.13 (2009) on traditional medicine, which requested the Director-General to update the previous 2002-2005 strategy. In May 2014 the Sixty-seventh World Health Assembly adopted resolution WHA67.18 urging Member States to adapt and implement it.

Two goals:
1. Harness the potential contribution of traditional and complementary medicine (T&CM) to health, wellness, people-centred health care and universal health coverage.
2. Promote the safe and effective use of T&CM through the regulation, research and integration of T&CM products, practices and practitioners into the health system, as appropriate.

Three strategic objectives (verified text):
1. To build the knowledge base for active management of T&CM through appropriate national policies.
2. To strengthen quality assurance, safety, proper use and effectiveness of T&CM by regulating products, practices and practitioners.
3. To promote universal health coverage by integrating T&CM services into health service delivery and self-health care.

Evidence of global uptake (WHO global report on T&CM 2019 and EB152/37 report):
- 170 Member States acknowledged use of traditional and complementary medicine.
- 98 countries reported a national policy on T&CM; 107 had a national T&CM office; 75 had a national research institute.
- Countries with a legal/regulatory framework for T&CM grew from 79 in 2012 to 109 in 2018.
- 124 countries reported laws or regulations on herbal medicines in 2018 (up from earlier years); 78 had practitioner regulations; 45 covered T&CM by health insurance.
- WHO International Clinical Trials Registry Platform listed 4,778 traditional medicine-related trials, including 165 restricted to COVID-19.
- T&CM outpatient service utilisation and pain management with T&CM are tracked as core health indicators.

WHO technical work relevant to quality: monographs on selected medicinal plants, guidelines on good herbal processing practices, selection of marker substances for quality control of herbal medicines, and a supporting chapter on Traditional Medicine Conditions in ICD-11. The International Regulatory Cooperation for Herbal Medicines (IRCH) supports convergence of herbal quality and safety regulation.

The strategy expired in 2023; the WHO is developing an updated traditional and complementary medicine strategy. India's Ayurveda, Yoga, Unani, Siddha and Sowa Rigpa systems are directly covered by WHO benchmarks for practice and training (e.g. Ayurveda, Panchakarma).
