WHO Traditional Medicine Strategy 2014–2023
The WHO Traditional Medicine Strategy 2014-2023 (ISBN 9789241506096) urges Member States to regulate, integrate and monitor traditional and complementary medicine (T&CM) products, practices and practitioners. Its strategic objectives are: building the knowledge base and forming national policies; strengthening safety, quality and effectiveness regulation; and promoting universal health coverage that integrates traditional medicine appropriately.

World Health Assembly resolutions
Resolution WHA62.13 (2009) on traditional medicine calls on Member States to formulate national policies and regulations for traditional medicine, to establish or strengthen regulatory mechanisms for herbal products, and to foster adoption of the WHO guidelines on good agricultural and collection practices (GACP) for medicinal plants. Resolution WHA67.18 (2014) endorses the WHO Traditional Medicine Strategy 2014-2023 and requests the Director-General to support Member States in assessing and integrating traditional and complementary medicine services.

WHO Global Report on Traditional and Complementary Medicine 2019
The WHO Global Report on T&CM 2019 documents regulation of T&CM across more than 170 Member States: growing numbers of countries have national policies, laws and frameworks for herbal medicines, and many regulate practitioners. The report highlights that the majority of Member States now have some regulatory framework and that WHO's role is to provide technical guidance for safe, effective and quality-assured traditional medicine.

Implication for Ayurveda IP
For export and regulatory strategy, WHO guidance implies supporting claims with references to authoritative monographs (e.g., WHO monographs on selected medicinal plants), compliance with GACP/GMP for herbal raw materials, and alignment of structure/function claim frameworks with national regulators (FSSAI, CDSCO, FDA, Health Canada).
