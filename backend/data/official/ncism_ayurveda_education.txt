TITLE: NCISM — National Commission for Indian System of Medicine, Ayurveda education and integrative practice
AUTHORITY: National Commission for Indian System of Medicine (NCISM), Ministry of AYUSH
JURISDICTION: India
SOURCE: https://ncismindia.org/
EFFECTIVE: 2021-onwards

Mandate
The National Commission for Indian System of Medicine (NCISM) was established under the National Commission for Indian System of Medicine Act, 2020, replacing the Central Council of Indian Medicine (CCIM). It regulates education, training and practice of the Indian System of Medicine (Ayurveda, Siddha, Unani, Sowa-Rigpa) in India and maintains a central register of practitioners.

Ayurveda undergraduate (BAMS) curriculum
The BAMS degree (Bachelor of Ayurvedic Medicine and Surgery) is the Ayurveda undergraduate course. The NCISM Ayush Graduate Medical Education (GME) regulations define the foundation course, duration (five and a half years including internship), and subject sequence: Padartha Vijnana, Sanskrit, Samhita subjects, Rachana and Kriya Sharir (anatomy and physiology), Dravyaguna Vijnana (pharmacology / materia medica), Rasa Shastra (pharmaceuticals), Swasthavritta (preventive and social medicine), Roga Nidan (diagnostics), Kayachikitsa (internal medicine), Shalya (surgery), Shalakya (ENT and ophthalmology), Prasuti and Stri Roga (obstetrics and gynaecology), and Bala Roga (paediatrics). Modern medical science subjects and an 80-credit evidence-based medicine / research methodology component are integrated.

Evidence-based practice and research
NCISM promotes an integrated, evidence-based approach: Ayurveda graduates are trained in modern diagnostic methods and research methodology so that Ayurveda practice is verifiable and documentable. Institutions conduct research under the Ministry of AYUSH framework, with support from the Central Council for Research in Ayurvedic Sciences (CCRAS).

Why this matters for an Innovation Passport
A clinical or research formulation linked to NCISM-recognised curricula, classical Samhita references, and published CCRAS/peer-reviewed research can cite educational and research authority in addition to classical texts, strengthening the evidentiary basis and reducing Section 3(p) risk by demonstrating a synergistic, evidence-supported technical effect.
